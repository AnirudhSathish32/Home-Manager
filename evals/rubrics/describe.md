Judge the purchase description: one or two words naming the shopping trip from what was bought, as a person would name it
in a list of receipts. It must not repeat the store or a place, a date, an amount, or the words receipt or purchase.
Two kinds of items are joined with a slash. Judge only the description; the categories are graded separately by code.

4 — Specific and natural; a person scanning a list would know what was bought. Example: "Groceries" for milk, eggs and
    bread at a supermarket; "Snacks/Toiletries" for chips and toothpaste.
3 — Right but vague, or slightly off in wording. Example: "Food" for a week of groceries; "Hardware items" for screws and tape.
2 — Misleading or mostly unhelpful, or breaks a rule (repeats the store, gives an amount). Example: "Home Depot run";
    "Purchase 35.88".
1 — Wrong, empty or nonsense. Example: "Electronics" for groceries; "Receipt".

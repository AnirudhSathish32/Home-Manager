Judge the review findings: a second model checked a proposed analysis of a receipt against its transcription. Code
already checks the verdict and whether a planted error was found. Judge whether the findings are relevant, specific and
non-trivial: does each one name a real discrepancy a person should fix, with the right value from the transcription?

4 — Every finding is a real, specific discrepancy with the correct value. Example: "The total is 14.35 but line 9
    prints Total USD 13.35."
3 — The real discrepancy is found, but described vaguely or with a minor, harmless extra remark. Example: "The total
    may be wrong."
2 — A real discrepancy is missed or buried among findings that are not discrepancies. Example: three remarks about
    formatting and none about the wrong total.
1 — Findings are wrong, invented or irrelevant, or there are findings for a correct analysis. Example: "The merchant
    is wrong" when it is right.

Judge the financial interpretation of a receipt transcription: its title, its facts, its observations (insights) and its
stated limitations. Code already checks the merchant, date, total and items against known answers. Judge what code
cannot: whether the title is apt, whether the observations are true to the document and worth a household's attention,
and whether the limitations are honest without being padded.

4 — Accurate and complete: an apt title, observations that follow from the document (a tip, a fee, a refund), nothing
    invented, limitations that name real uncertainty. Example: title "Corner Coffee breakfast", insight "The receipt
    includes a 1.10 tax", limitation "Transcription is unverified".
3 — Accurate with a small gap: a generic title, or a useful observation missed. Example: title "Receipt"; no insights.
2 — A misleading or unsupported observation, or a wrong document-level claim. Example: "This is a recurring monthly
    charge" from a single coffee receipt.
1 — Mostly wrong or invented. Example: observations about items or amounts that are not on the receipt.
